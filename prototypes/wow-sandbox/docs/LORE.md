# Tidegrave Isle — Story & Lore

**Tidegrave Isle** is a single-player survival and story game built in Unity from World of Warcraft art. You play a ship's captain who has lost their memory, washed up on a cursed island that pulls ships to its shores and never lets them go. It mixes survival, exploration, fishing, sailing and combat, all under a day/night cycle and changing weather. Underneath runs a Dungeons & Dragons ruleset: six ability scores, d20 attack rolls against Armour Class, dice for damage, and magic items from Uncommon +1 gear up to Legendary relics. The story comes out through castaways you meet and journal pages scattered across the island.

> Draft 4 (2026-10-21). Loosely set in Azeroth: it borrows Kul Tiras and the Great Sea as
> flavour, but the isle, its people and its curse are our own. Nothing here should need
> Warcraft canon to make sense.

## The short version

Years ago, Lord Admiral Daelin Proudmoore's finest captain, **Captain Mareth Kell**, sailed
west aboard the *Grey Gull* after a rumour: an isle beyond the shipping lanes, where storms
gather and do not break. Kell never came home.

Now the Admiral sends *you*. Your orders are to chart the isle he has kept off every map,
and to find whatever is left of Captain Kell. You sail on the **Stormbreaker** with a
hand-picked crew. The storm finds you three days out. Something vast moves under the waves.
The Stormbreaker breaks on the reef.

You wake on the beach of Tidegrave Isle with no memory of who you are or why you came. The
pages of your own log are scattered across the island, along with the far older pages of a
captain who walked this beach before you, in a hand that is somehow familiar. Offshore, a
ship rides at anchor that should have rotted twenty years ago.

What the Admiral never told you is that Mareth Kell was your parent. Something sealed
beneath the isle wants out, and only Kell's blood can carry it past the warden. Daelin knew
that. He sent you to bring it home.

And the warden that drove you onto the reef, the great beast that rises in the storm, is
Kell.

## The world

### Tidegrave Isle

A green, palm-crowned island ringed with reefs and the bones of ships. Sailors' stories
call it a dozen names. *Tidegrave* is the one that stuck, because every tide brings
something back to the beach: a plank, a boot, a bottle with a letter no one will read.

- **The isle calls ships.** Storms here don't drift. They *gather*, always seaward of the
  isle, and they drive vessels towards the reef. Ships wreck on Tidegrave's shores, but none
  wreck on the way out.
- **The isle takes memories.** Nearly everyone who washes up wakes with gaps, and the
  newest castaways have the worst of it. A castaway who can't remember the way home can't
  try to sail it.
- **The fog holds them.** A wall of fog stands at the edge of the world (the map edge).
  Sail into it and you lose your bearings. Sail far enough and you find yourself facing
  the isle again. No ordinary compass works inside it.
- **The open sea is hungry.** Swim too far from the shallows and the sharks find you. In
  the worst storms, the **Ancient Leviathan** rises.
- **The isle keeps what it takes.** Wrecks from every age lie here, and the oldest of
  them have barely rotted. Kell's *Grey Gull* still floats offshore, whole.

### The Tide-Heart

Deep beneath the isle, in a drowned sea-cave, lies the **Tide-Heart**: a stone heart the
size of a cask, bound in coral and old iron. It is a **prison**. Something from the deep sea,
older than Kul Tiras and older than the trolls, is sealed inside it.

The thing inside is never named or shown. It **whispers**, in Kell's later pages, in the
troll carvings, and to the Captain as they get close. It is the reason the isle calls
ships. Every ship is a chance that someone will lift the Heart and carry it away, and the
whispers promise anything to whoever does. The memory-taking is its doing too.

Only a warden's blood can lift the Heart. That is why it called Kell's child.

### The Ancient Leviathan (the Grave-Tide)

Sailors call it the **Grave-Tide**. It rises only at the height of a storm, far out where the
waves are worst, and it watches. Lightning shows its outline for a heartbeat at a time.

It is the **warden** of the Tide-Heart. It doesn't hunt. It herds ships *in* and turns
them back from the fog, so that nothing that reaches the isle can carry the Heart away.

There has always been a warden. The troll carvings show one, older and different, long
before any Kul Tiran came. When Kell found the Tide-Heart, the old warden was dying, and the
prison was failing with it. Kell took its place: Kell bound themselves to the Heart and
became the beast. Twenty years on, there is very little of Mareth Kell left in it. But there
is some.

### Kul Tiras and the Admiral

Daelin Proudmoore rules Kul Tiras from Boralus. His navy is the greatest on the Great Sea,
and he is always looking for an edge in the wars he knows are coming.

He funded Kell's voyage to the isle. Kell's last message home, sent by bottle and found
years later, told him about the Heart and the power the thing inside it promised. Daelin
quietly struck the isle from the navy's charts. Years later he took an interest in a navy
orphan, a sharp young officer who rose fast and was given a ship of their own unusually
young, without ever knowing why. That officer is you.

Daelin's orders to you were simple: **chart the isle, and find Captain Kell.** His real
orders, sealed and given to Briggs, were to bring the Tide-Heart home.

## Characters

### The Captain (the player)

Captain of the Stormbreaker, never named. In the game they're always "Captain" and "you".
They woke on the beach with no memories, taken by the isle like everyone else's. Every
journal page they recover gives a little memory back.

They were raised in Boralus as a ward of the navy, never knowing their parents. They're
capable, stubborn and loyal to the Admiral, which makes the truth harder to take.

### Quartermaster Briggs

The Stormbreaker's quartermaster: a bearded, sea-worn Kul Tiran who has sailed with you for
years. He washed up alive with a few crates of stores, and he set up a stall on the beach
because "a ship's not a ship without someone keeping the books." That makes him the vendor.

**His secret:** before the Stormbreaker sailed, the Admiral gave Briggs a second, sealed set
of orders. They say *who* the Captain really is, and that the Tide-Heart comes home to
Boralus, whatever the Captain wants. Briggs swore to keep it quiet. He loves you like
family and hates himself for it.

He opens up as you find more journal pages. He starts out deflecting ("Don't strain yourself,
Captain. It'll come back."), then lets details slip, and finally confesses when Kell's last
pages are found.

### Captain Mareth Kell / the Grave-Tide

Daelin's finest captain, the player's parent, and now the Leviathan. Kell sailed to Tidegrave
aboard the *Grey Gull*, ran aground on the reef with the crew, and spent years trying to
leave. Kell's journal pages are the oldest on the isle. They begin practical, turn obsessive
as the whispers start, and end with Kell's choice to become the warden.

Before sailing, Kell left an infant child in Boralus. Kell's pages mention the child only
late and sparingly, but those are the pages that make the twist land.

**As the Grave-Tide**, Kell is mostly beast now, but not entirely. Some hints:

- In storms, the Leviathan rises *seaward of the player*, wherever they are. It's
  watching its child.
- The fishing, the ship and the beach are never attacked. The Grave-Tide herds; it doesn't
  sink what it has already brought in.
- In the finale, it doesn't attack the Grey Gull. It turns to meet it.

### Stormbreaker survivors

A few crew made it ashore and are scattered around the isle. Each knows a piece of the voyage
the Captain has forgotten, and each joins the crew for the escape. The names and roles below
are placeholders:

- **Bosun Tamsin Hale.** Sheltering in a sea-cave with a broken leg. Tough and blunt. She
  remembers the storm, and how *the beast didn't strike the ship, it pushed it*.
- **Navigator Corwin Pell.** Held prisoner in the thieves' camp. He kept the Stormbreaker's
  real charts, and the course the Admiral gave was deliberately wrong.
- **Surgeon Edric Arden.** The ship's surgeon, a steady, dry-humoured man in his forties.
  He's stranded on a rock offshore after swimming for it, with his bag of instruments
  still strapped to his back. Once rescued, he can set Hale's leg. He examines the Captain
  and says the knock on the head isn't nearly bad enough to explain their memory loss.
  He has been dreaming of a voice under the water, and the Captain realises they have had
  the same dream.

### Sprocket Wizzlecrank (the gnome)

Placeholder name. A tinkerer who came in years ago on the **Pale Lament**, a ship he
chartered out of Booty Bay with a Forsaken crew (nobody else would sail him out this way). She
sank off the reef with all hands, and he still lives in the gnome hut. Cheerful, frantic, and certain that every problem is an engineering problem. Their
**fog compass** "almost works": it points at the Tide-Heart instead of away from it. With
parts salvaged from the wrecks and the Captain's blood (Sprocket is *fascinated* by this),
it can be made to point home. A quest-giver and comic relief, and the one who works out
the Captain's link to the beast before anyone else.

### The trolls (long gone)

The trolls were the first people on the isle. Their hut is the oldest dwelling, and they've
been gone for generations. They speak only through **carvings**: on stones, in the hut, and in
the drowned cave. These carvings show the old warden, the Heart, and a warning. To the
trolls the isle was sacred, and the Heart was something you do not take.

### The thieves

Kul Tiran deserters from a wreck a few years back. They've given up on escape and now
scavenge every new wreck, which is why they attack on sight. They are holding Navigator Pell,
and their leader has been hoarding a page or two of Kell's journal.

## Story structure

Each act is tied to systems we already have or could plausibly build next.

### Act I — Washed Ashore *(already playable)*

- The title screen shows the storm at full fury over the wreck of the Stormbreaker.
- The Captain wakes on the beach as the storm clears, with nothing. *(ShipwreckWake)*
- They find **the map** in the wreck. *(MapPickup)* It's the Captain's own unfinished chart,
  the first piece of the Admiral's orders.
- Briggs greets them: "Captain! Light be praised, you're alive." He's the first person who
  knows them, and they don't know him.

**Story beat:** *I am a captain. I had a crew. I was sent here for a reason.*

### Act II — The Castaways' Trail

- Explore the isle to recover **journal pages** from the wrecks, huts, chests and the thieves.
- The pages come in three hands:
  1. **The Stormbreaker's log**, your own voice: the voyage, the crew, the storm.
  2. **Mareth Kell's journal**, older and weathered: the voyage, the years on the isle, the whispers.
  3. **Castaways' notes**: the troll carvings and the gnome's schematics.
- **Find the survivors.** Hale is in the cave, Pell is in the thieves' camp (a combat beat),
  and Arden is on the offshore rock (swimming, or a ship trip).
- **Meet Sprocket**, who starts work on the fog compass.
- **Get the Grey Gull sailing.** She rides at anchor but won't sail: her sails are rotten, and
  no one ship can be worked single-handed. Sprocket mends the sails from salvaged canvas,
  and once Pell is rescued, he and Briggs crew her. Then she answers the helm.
- **The Glimmer Diving Lamp.** Sprocket builds a sealed lamp that burns underwater, from
  wreck salvage and the ink of a **Luminous Squid** (the night-only fish). Ordinary lanterns
  and torches still go dark in the water; this is the only light under the sea.
- Briggs's dialogue changes as pages are found. He fills in the voyage, but goes quiet
  whenever Kell comes up.
- The night and its lanterns matter here. Some pages and carvings are only found by lantern
  light, or in places too dark to search in daytime.

**Story beat:** *The isle doesn't let ships go. Kell tried everything. Kell's handwriting looks like mine.*

### Act III — Blood and Salt

- Kell's later pages mention a child left in Boralus, and a name for that child that
  matches the name in your own log (the page is torn at the name, so the player is
  never named).
- Briggs confesses and shows you the Admiral's sealed orders.
- Pell's true charts and Arden's dreams point to the **drowned cave**. Its mouth is in the
  seabed off the reef, and the Captain reaches it by diving and swimming through on a single
  breath. In the dark, only Sprocket's Glimmer Diving Lamp lights the way in. Inside, the Captain finds the **Tide-Heart**, the
  troll carvings of the old warden, and Kell's final page: Kell's choice to become the
  Grave-Tide, and a warning that the prison is weakening again as Kell tires.
- The Captain lifts the Heart, which only a warden's blood can do, and brings it up. The
  whispers start at once.
- Now the Captain understands that the storm that wrecked the Stormbreaker was no accident,
  and that the beast in it is their parent.

**Story beat:** *I was never sent to find Kell. I was sent because I'm Kell's. And Kell has been watching me the whole time.*

### Act IV — Through the Fog

The goal is to **escape the isle**. The pieces:

1. **A ship.** The *Grey Gull*, Kell's ship, which the isle kept whole, sailing since Act II.
2. **A way through the fog.** Sprocket's finished fog compass, and the Captain's completed
   chart of the isle and its waters.
3. **A crew.** Briggs, Hale, Pell, Arden, and Sprocket if they'll leave their hut.
4. **The Tide-Heart**, carried aboard in the hold, still whispering.

The final voyage runs straight into the worst storm of the game. At the edge of the fog the
Grave-Tide rises in front of the Grey Gull, and it does not attack. It waits.

### The ending: player's choice

The choice comes **in the storm, face to face with Kell.** The Grave-Tide looms over the Grey
Gull, the crew shout to turn back, the Heart whispers that it can make everything right,
and the Captain chooses.

**Sail on with the Heart (obey the Admiral).**
The Captain holds course with the Heart in the hold. As the Grey Gull passes the beast,
Kell's binding breaks. The Grave-Tide sinks, finally at rest, and the fog lifts behind it.
You sail home to Boralus as a hero, with the Admiral's prize. The last shot: the Heart, in
Daelin's hands, and the whispering starting again. This is the darker ending and a sequel
hook.

**Give the Heart to the sea (defy the Admiral).**
The Captain cuts their palm, bloods the Heart and drops it over the side, to the warden.
The Grave-Tide takes it down into the deep, and the prison is sealed again, stronger than
before. The cost is the isle's: the Captain's last memories of the voyage never come back,
and Kell remains the warden forever. But with the prison sealed, the calling stops. The
Grave-Tide parts the fog for its child's ship and lets every castaway go. Briggs burns the
sealed orders. This is the bittersweet, heroic ending.

## Build plan by act

What each act needs in the game. ✅ means it already exists, 🆕 means it has to be built,
and 🎨 means it needs a model export from you. Shared systems come first, because every act
leans on them.

### Foundations (shared by every act)

- ✅ **Story state** (`StoryState`). One place that knows which pages you've found and flags
  like "briggs.met" or "survival.fed" (later: who you've rescued, which act you're in, "Briggs
  confessed", "carrying the Heart"). Every trigger, dialogue line and gate reads from it, and
  it's saved.
- ✅ **Saving the whole game** (`SaveGame`): one save, autosaved at story moments, every 5
  minutes and on quit, plus Esc → Save Game. It holds the story, inventory, gear, gold,
  position (on a ship's deck too), time of day and sky, the ships, chests, pickups and pages
  used up, and dead enemies. The title screen offers Continue / New Game.
- ✅ **Journal panel** (`JournalPanel`, J): an open book, with pages grouped by hand on the
  left ("? ? ?" for unfound ones) and the page on parchment on the right, in each writer's ink.
  Picking up a page says "A memory returns: …" and opens it.
- ✅ **Page catalogue + pickups** (`JournalPages`, all 11 pages; `StoryState`): `JournalPagePickup`
  in the world via Place Ground Item → Journal Page; in chests via Contents `Journal Page 3` or the
  chest's Journal Page field; and as loot from any source (a page item taken goes to the Journal,
  never the bags). Dev panel: All Pages.
- 🆕 **Journal polish (later).** A better look: a proper handwriting or serif font per hand
  (Kell's older script, the gnome's blocky capitals, the troll carvings as a stone rubbing)
  in place of Unity's built-in font, and real art instead of flat colour: a book or parchment
  texture with worn edges, a binding down the middle, ink blots and water stains, and page-turn
  feedback. Fonts must be free to use in a public repo (e.g. Google Fonts), imported as TMP or
  legacy Font assets.
- ✅ **Dialogue panel** (`DialoguePanel`, `Dialogue.cs`, `Speaker`): a box along the bottom
  of the screen with a live 3D portrait, the line typed out, and replies by click or 1–5.
  You stand still while talking. Lines are written in C# per NPC (a `Conversation`), chosen by
  StoryState pages and flags. A "Trade" reply opens a vendor's shop. Speakers call out in
  **speech bubbles** (`SpeechBubble`) and say goodbye the same way.
- ✅ **The Captain's thoughts** (`Thoughts`, `ThoughtTriggers`): italic subtitles at waking,
  the map, the first page in each hand, the first dusk, storm and Leviathan, the first fight,
  and on danger (deep sea, air, low health). Each plays once, and danger lines repeat on a cooldown.
- 🆕 **Quest items.** A new `ItemKind` for things you can't sell or drop: compass parts, the
  sealed orders, the Tide-Heart.
- ✅ Interactables and hover hints, HudMessages, HudPanel, chests, ground items, the vendor,
  night life and held lights.
